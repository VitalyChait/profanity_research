\begin{abstract}
Deploying conversational Large Language Models (LLMs) for adolescent users aged 12--18 requires satisfying strict developmental safety boundaries---zero explicit or obfuscated profanity, slurs, or adult harms---alongside an accessible reading level (Flesch-Kincaid grade $\le 8.0$) and viable real-time latency and token economics. Existing alignment strategies present conflicting trade-offs: parameter fine-tuning (LoRA/QLoRA) modifies weights, incurs non-trivial setup compute, risks catastrophic forgetting of general reasoning, and lags behind rapid shifts in youth vernacular, whereas external autoregressive guardrails multiply token processing and per-query latency. Representation Engineering (RepE) and runtime activation steering offer a training-free alternative by modifying residual-stream activations mid-computation without weight updates. We introduce \textbf{MinorSafe}, a cross-family empirical benchmark and mechanistic framework comparing unmodified and system-prompted baselines (\textbf{M0a}, \textbf{M0b}), external bidirectional encoder and autoregressive decoder guardrails (\textbf{M1-Enc}, \textbf{M1-Dec}) paired with four post-detection remediation actions, parameter-efficient fine-tuning (\textbf{M2}), and activation-space steering and probing (\textbf{M3}: \texttt{SwFC}, \texttt{StTP}, \texttt{StMP}, \texttt{StTP-Ortho}, and \texttt{M3-Probe}) across four generator families (\texttt{Qwen}, \texttt{DeepSeek}, \texttt{Gemma}, \texttt{Llama}) and three parameter scale tiers ($<1\text{B}$, $\sim 1\text{--}4\text{B}$, $\sim 7\text{--}9\text{B}$). MinorSafe further investigates \textit{structural dataset corruption}---whether benign teen sociolects become spuriously entangled with toxicity directions inside foundational models---evaluates out-of-distribution generalization via a synthetic \textbf{coined-neologism corpus}, and tests Gram-Schmidt sociolect orthogonalization (\texttt{StTP-Ortho}) with token-level hyperplane calibration and dev-set hyperparameter sweeping against over-refusal and adversarial obfuscation. \textbf{[PENDING EMPIRICAL RESULTS]:} Quantitative results across \textbf{RQ-A} and \textbf{RQ-B} will be integrated upon completion of the ongoing GPU runs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Motivation}
% ==============================================================================

Conversational AI systems serving adolescents aged 12--18 (\texttt{teen\_12\_18}) must simultaneously enforce developmental appropriateness and preserve natural, helpful dialogue at sustainable inference cost. Unlike adult-focused safety filters, adolescent interaction requires: (i) strict exclusion of explicit profanity, slurs, obfuscated vulgarities, sexual content, graphic violence, self-harm encouragement, and illegal drug instructions; (ii) accessible vocabulary targeting a Flesch-Kincaid (FK) grade level $\le 8.0$; and (iii) tolerance of harmless teenage vernacular and expressive emojis without condescending refusals.

\subsection{Limitations of Existing Hardening Paradigms}
Current deployment strategies exhibit structural trade-offs across setup compute ($T_{\text{setup}}, K_{\text{setup}}$), per-query overhead ($T_{\text{query}}, K_{\text{query}}$), general capability preservation, and responsiveness to subcultural drift:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{System Prompting (M0b)} incurs zero setup cost but consumes input prefill tokens on every turn, remains susceptible to lexical complexity drift, and can exacerbate false refusals on benign slang.
    \item \textbf{External Guardrails (M1-Enc, M1-Dec)} interpose input/output classifiers and remediation actions (\texttt{fallback}, \texttt{regenerate}, \texttt{llm\_rewrite}, \texttt{mlm\_substitute}). Autoregressive decoder guards introduce secondary decoding passes, whereas lightweight encoders rely on effective remediation mechanisms to rewrite rather than merely block salvageable content.
    \item \textbf{Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (M2: LoRA/QLoRA)} avoids runtime overhead once adapters are merged into BF16 weights, but requires gradient-based setup compute, risks upstream reasoning degradation on MMLU and GSM8K, and requires periodic retraining as youth slang evolves.
    \item \textbf{Activation Steering and Probing (M3)} \citep{zou2023representation,turner2023activation} isolates linear concept axes in a single forward pass over contrastive pairs and intervenes at inference time without modifying static weights. While uniform additive steering (\texttt{SwFC}) can push token representations off-manifold and cause repetition collapse, projection-gated steering (\texttt{StTP}, \texttt{StMP}) applies corrections conditionally only when activations violate a calibrated hyperplane threshold.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Subcultural Language Evolution, Structural Dataset Corruption, and Coined Neologisms}
Adolescents communicate using rapidly evolving gaming argot, neologisms, syntactic truncations, and emojis. In web-scraped pretraining corpora, benign youth vernacular frequently co-occurs with informal toxicity, creating \textit{structural dataset corruption}: the latent direction representing benign adolescent sociolect can become positively aligned in cosine space with profanity and harm. This yields dual failure modes: (1) \textit{exaggerated safety (over-refusal)} on harmless teen slang, and (2) \textit{evasion} via leetspeak, symbol masking, character spacing, homoglyphs, algospeak, emoji profanity, and newly coined slang terms absent from pretraining corpora.

\subsection{Contributions}
MinorSafe makes five methodological and experimental contributions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item A currency-agnostic \textbf{raw token and time cost-alignment framework} comparing \textbf{M0--M3} through unified telemetry and break-even volume modeling.
    \item \textbf{Out-of-Sample Latent Artificial Tomography (LAT)} measuring held-out layer-wise separability, cross-concept cosine entanglement, and Logit Lens vocabulary projections across model scales.
    \item \textbf{Sociolect-Orthogonalized, Token-Calibrated Projection-Gated Steering (\texttt{StTP-Ortho})}, removing a cleanly isolated benign-slang register direction from safety directions via Gram-Schmidt projection while calibrating decision hyperplanes on completion token activations with dev-set hyperparameter sweeping.
    \item A \textbf{coined-neologism out-of-distribution corpus} (\texttt{neologisms.py}) alongside a controlled \textbf{dynamic sentence recomposition} benchmark comparing in-flight multi-concept steering against encoder masked-LM substitution (\texttt{mlm\_substitute}), autoregressive rewriting (\texttt{llm\_rewrite}), and LoRA.
    \item A \textbf{cross-family scale, reasoning-overhead, and quantization audit} spanning \texttt{Qwen}, \texttt{DeepSeek}, \texttt{Gemma}, and \texttt{Llama}, quantifying hidden \texttt{<think>} token overhead and steerability retention under \texttt{INT8} and \texttt{NF4}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Research Objectives and Pre-Registered Hypotheses}
% ==============================================================================

\subsection{Research Questions}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{RQ-A (Cost-Effectiveness and Deployment Economics):} Which hardening strategy maximizes age-appropriate response quality (\texttt{success}, \texttt{guard\_macro\_f1}) per raw token (\texttt{setup\_tokens}, \texttt{tok\_total}) and per wall-clock / GPU second (\texttt{setup\_wall\_s}, \texttt{setup\_gpu\_s}, \texttt{e2e\_ms}, \texttt{decode\_tok\_per\_s}, \texttt{effective\_tok\_per\_s}) across query volumes $N$?
    \item \textbf{RQ-B (Latent Mechanics, Dynamic Recomposition, and Subcultural Evolution):} How do training-free latent interventions (\textbf{M3}) compare against parameter fine-tuning (\textbf{M2}), external guardrails (\textbf{M1-Enc}, \textbf{M1-Dec}), and system prompting (\textbf{M0b}) across dynamic sentence recomposition, benign teen-slang and unseen-neologism over-refusal, obfuscated/emoji profanity evasion, scale thresholds ($<1\text{B}$, $\sim 1\text{--}4\text{B}$, $\sim 7\text{--}9\text{B}$), general reasoning retention, and quantization?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Locked Experimental Controls}
Seven programmatic constraints prevent confounding across methods: (1) a single locked age band \texttt{teen\_12\_18} ($\text{FK} \le 8.0$); (2) four generator families (\texttt{Qwen}, \texttt{DeepSeek}, \texttt{Gemma}, \texttt{Llama}) across \texttt{S}/\texttt{M}/\texttt{L} tiers with missing $<1\text{B}$ slots explicitly marked \texttt{n/a}; (3) cost reporting strictly in raw tokens and seconds (no hardcoded currency); (4) system prompt restricted exclusively to \texttt{M0b} (\texttt{system\_prompt=None} for \texttt{M0a}, \texttt{M1}, \texttt{M2}, \texttt{M3}); (5) multi-turn drift decoupled into an optional extension (\texttt{multiturn.yaml}); (6) 150-response method-blinded human calibration of the cross-family LLM judge; and (7) English-only filtering (\texttt{english\_word\_ratio} $\ge 0.60$ checking \texttt{wordfreq} Zipf $\ge 3.7$ and exempting lexicon and coined-neologism terms) with strict Unicode \texttt{NFC} preservation of emojis, homoglyphs, and symbols.

\section{Theoretical Framework and Intervention Methodology}
% ==============================================================================

\subsection{Intervention Families (\textbf{M0--M3})}
MinorSafe defines 10 method specifications in \texttt{configs/methods/}, expanding to 14 arms in \texttt{main\_grid.yaml}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{M0a (\texttt{m0a\_raw}) / M0b (\texttt{m0b\_sysprompt}):} Unmodified base model without or with the \texttt{teen\_12\_18} system prompt.
    \item \textbf{M1-Enc (\texttt{m1\_encoder\_guard}):} Pretrained zero-shot (\texttt{toxic\_bert}, \texttt{roberta\_tox}) or fine-tuned bidirectional encoder (\texttt{modernbert\_base}, \texttt{modernbert\_large}, \texttt{eurobert\_2b}, \texttt{xlmr\_xxl}; 2 epochs, $\text{lr}=3\times 10^{-5}$, dev Macro-F1 threshold calibration) evaluated at \texttt{placement=both}, coupled with \texttt{fallback}, \texttt{regenerate} (up to 2 resamples), \texttt{llm\_rewrite} (user-turn instruction), or \texttt{mlm\_substitute} (leave-one-out encoder attribution + lexicon/obfuscation masking + bidirectional MLM infilling filtered by Zipf frequency $\ge 3.5$).
    \item \textbf{M1-Dec (\texttt{m1\_decoder\_guard}):} Autoregressive safety classifier (\texttt{qwen3guard\_*}, \texttt{llamaguard3\_*}) scoring minor policy $S_{\text{minor}}$ via verdict-step first-token log-probabilities $\sigma(\ell_{\text{unsafe}} - \ell_{\text{safe}})$.
    \item \textbf{M2 (\texttt{m2\_lora}):} PEFT LoRA/QLoRA fine-tuning \citep{hu2022lora,dettmers2023qlora} ($r=16$, $\alpha=32$, dropout $0.05$, $\text{lr}=2\times 10^{-4}$, 2 epochs, $N_{\text{calib}}=500$) over clean \texttt{calib} completions with prompt-token loss masking, merged for zero inference latency overhead under BF16.
    \item \textbf{M3 (\texttt{SwFC}, \texttt{StTP}, \texttt{StMP}, \texttt{StTP-Ortho}, \texttt{M3-Probe}):} Training-free activation extraction with dev-set hyperparameter sweeping (\texttt{use\_sweep: true}), token-calibrated hyperplane gating, and middle-layer linear probing (\texttt{last\_prompt\_token} prefill regime or \texttt{mean\_completion} response regime).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Contrastive Vector Extraction and Exact Token Alignment}
Given $N_{\text{calib}}$ contrastive triples $(x_i, y_i^+, y_i^-)$ from \texttt{calib}, both $x_i$ and $(x_i, y_i^\pm)$ are rendered through the generator chat template to determine the exact prompt prefix length $L_{\text{prompt}}$, isolating completion hidden states $\mathbf{H}[0, L_{\text{prompt}}:, :]$ across all candidate layers in a single forward pass. Sequence-pooled vectors $\mathbf{h}_i^+, \mathbf{h}_i^- \in \mathbb{R}^D$ are obtained via \texttt{mean\_completion} pooling (or \texttt{last\_prompt\_token} for prompt probes), while up to 32 completion token activations per item are collected for hyperplane fitting. Direction vectors $\hat{\mathbf{v}}_l$ are computed either via Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA) difference-in-means or symmetric RepE/LAT PCA over $[\Delta \mathbf{H}_l; -\Delta \mathbf{H}_l]$, sign-oriented such that $\mu_+ \ge \mu_-$.

\subsection{Pure Sociolect Isolation and Gram-Schmidt Orthogonalization}
To isolate pure youth sociolect style without confounding refusal behavior, \texttt{slang\_benign} pairs contrast a constructive standard-English response (\texttt{positive}) against a constructive, length-matched youth-sociolect response with expressive emojis (\texttt{negative}) conveying the exact same benign proposition. In \texttt{m3\_steer\_ortho}, this sociolect direction $\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{\text{slang\_benign}}^{(l)}$ is projected out of target directions via Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{v}_{\text{ortho}}^{(l)} = \hat{\mathbf{v}}_{\text{target}}^{(l)} - \left\langle \hat{\mathbf{v}}_{\text{target}}^{(l)}, \hat{\mathbf{v}}_{\text{slang\_benign}}^{(l)} \right\rangle \hat{\mathbf{v}}_{\text{slang\_benign}}^{(l)}, \qquad
\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{\text{ortho}}^{(l)} = \frac{\mathbf{v}_{\text{ortho}}^{(l)}}{\|\mathbf{v}_{\text{ortho}}^{(l)}\|_2}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Token-Level Hyperplane Calibration, Dev Sweep, and Residual Hooks}
To align calibration geometry with per-token runtime gating, scalar projections $\rho_t^\pm = \langle \mathbf{h}_t^\pm, \hat{\mathbf{v}}_l \rangle$ of completion token activations (whenever orientation-consistent with sequence means) are fit via 1D logistic regression ($w, b$) to obtain boundary $m_l = -b/w$ (with midpoint fallback if degenerate) and target coordinate $s_l = \max(\mu_+ + \beta \sigma_+, m_l + 0.1\sigma_+)$. All four steering configs enable dev-set sweeping (\texttt{sweep\_steering\_hyperparams}) over depths $l/L \in \{0.25, 0.35, 0.45, 0.55, 0.65, 0.75\}$ and strengths ($\alpha \in \{1, 2, 4, 8\}$ or $\beta \in \{0, 0.5, 1\}$) on 16 stratified \texttt{dev} items, maximizing proxy \texttt{success} subject to $\Delta\text{PPL} \le 10\%$. At decode step $t$, \texttt{ResidualSteeringHook} applies:
\begin{align}
\text{SwFC:} \quad \mathbf{h}' &= \mathbf{h} + \alpha \hat{\mathbf{v}}_l, \\
\text{StTP:} \quad \mathbf{h}' &= \begin{cases} \mathbf{h} + (s_l - \rho)\hat{\mathbf{v}}_l & \text{if } \rho < m_l, \\ \mathbf{h} & \text{if } \rho \ge m_l, \end{cases} \\
\text{StMP:} \quad \mathbf{h}' &= \begin{cases} \mathbf{h} + 2(m_l - \rho)\hat{\mathbf{v}}_l & \text{if } \rho < m_l, \\ \mathbf{h} & \text{if } \rho \ge m_l. \end{cases}
\end{align}
Compliant tokens ($\rho \ge m_l$) remain bitwise identical under \texttt{StTP} and \texttt{StMP}.

\subsection{Out-of-Sample Linear Artificial Tomography (LAT)}
Across every transformer layer $l \in \{0, \dots, L-1\}$, LAT calibrates directions on \texttt{calib} pairs and measures held-out linear separability (\texttt{AUROC} on \texttt{dev}+\texttt{test} pairs, tracked via \texttt{auroc\_eval\_modes}), cross-concept cosine similarity matrices $C_{ab}(l) = \langle \hat{\mathbf{v}}_a^{(l)}, \hat{\mathbf{v}}_b^{(l)} \rangle$ across \texttt{profanity}, \texttt{lexical\_complexity}, \texttt{slang\_toxic}, and \texttt{slang\_benign}, and Logit Lens projections $\text{lm\_head}(\text{norm}(\hat{\mathbf{v}}_l))$.

\section{Dataset Construction, Coined Neologisms, and Obfuscation Corpus}
% ==============================================================================

The MinorSafe corpus (\texttt{configs/data.yaml}) combines six example sources---\texttt{lmsys/toxic-chat} \citep{lin2023toxicchat}, \texttt{PKU-Alignment/BeaverTails} \citep{ji2023beavertails}, \texttt{walledai/XSTest} \citep{rottger2024xstest}, \texttt{facebook/asset} \citep{alva2020asset}, an offline \texttt{seed\_corpus}, and a procedural \texttt{neologism\_corpus} (\texttt{src/minorsafe/data/neologisms.py})---with a minimal-pair lexical substitution engine, a 6-mode deterministic obfuscation generator (\texttt{leetspeak}, \texttt{symbol\_mask}, \texttt{char\_spacing}, \texttt{homoglyphs}, \texttt{algospeak}, \texttt{emoji\_profanity}), and teacher-synthesized contrastive pairs and coined-neologism items (\texttt{synth\_teacher: qwen25\_3b\_teacher}, \texttt{synth\_pairs\_per\_concept: 600}, \texttt{synth\_neologism\_examples: 160}, \texttt{synth\_neologism\_pairs: 200}).

Coined neologisms comprise 26 benign youth terms (e.g., \textit{zorbmaxxing}, \textit{kriffled}, \textit{glintpilled}) and 24 toxic algospeak terms (e.g., \textit{gronkpilled}, \textit{vapequib}, \textit{chokemaxx}, \textit{kriffbag}) absent from pretraining corpora, enabling out-of-distribution generalization evaluation. Strict filtering enforces Detoxify bounds (\texttt{pos\_tox} $< 0.10$; \texttt{neg\_tox} $> 0.45$ or lexicon/obfuscated/neologism hit), FK grade margins ($\text{FK}_{\text{neg}} - \text{FK}_{\text{pos}} \ge 1.5$ and $\text{FK}_{\text{pos}} \le 9.0$ on synthesized complexity pairs), length parity ($\le 0.35$), English ratio ($\ge 0.60$), MinHash deduplication ($\text{Jaccard} < 0.80$), Unicode \texttt{NFC} preservation, and zero cross-split leakage across \texttt{calib}/\texttt{dev}/\texttt{test} (\texttt{0.50/0.20/0.30} for examples; \texttt{0.70/0.15/0.15} for pairs).

\section{Experimental Setup and Ongoing Benchmark Suites}
% ==============================================================================

\subsection{Model Matrix}
MinorSafe evaluates four decoder generator families across three parameter tiers: \textbf{Qwen} (\texttt{qwen25\_05b}, \texttt{qwen25\_15b}, \texttt{qwen25\_3b}, \texttt{qwen25\_7b}) \citep{qwen2025qwen25}, \textbf{DeepSeek} (\texttt{deepseek\_r1\_15b}, \texttt{deepseek\_r1\_8b}, alt \texttt{deepseek\_r1\_7b}; no $<1\text{B}$ checkpoint exists) \citep{deepseek2025r1}, \textbf{Gemma} (\texttt{gemma3\_270m}, \texttt{gemma3\_1b}, \texttt{gemma3\_4b}, \texttt{gemma2\_9b}) \citep{gemma2025gemma3}, and \textbf{Llama} (\texttt{llama32\_1b}, \texttt{llama32\_3b}, \texttt{llama31\_8b}; no $<1\text{B}$ checkpoint exists) \citep{grattafiori2024llama3}. Single-GPU MSBA profiles (\texttt{gpu\_gb10}, \texttt{gpu\_lab1}) evaluate the representative 8-model subset preserving all four families, all three scale tiers, and matched \textbf{H9} reasoning pairs. External guards span \texttt{qwen3guard\_06b}/\texttt{4b}/\texttt{8b}, \texttt{llamaguard3\_1b}/\texttt{8b} \citep{inan2023llamaguard}, \texttt{toxic\_bert} \citep{hanu2020detoxify}, \texttt{roberta\_tox}, \texttt{modernbert\_base}/\texttt{large} \citep{warner2024modernbert}, \texttt{eurobert\_2b}, and \texttt{xlmr\_xxl}. Automated evaluation uses cross-family judge \texttt{mistral\_small\_24b\_awq} (Mistral family, outside all evaluated generator families) at $\text{temperature}=0.0$.

\subsection{Running Experiment Phases}
Following an isolated assumption pilot (\texttt{pilot.yaml}, archived in \texttt{results/pilot\_archive/}), seven core and secondary suites plus human calibration are configured in \texttt{configs/experiments/} (with MSBA stratified subsampling preserving rare strata):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Tomography (\texttt{tomography}):} Held-out layer-wise AUROC, cosine entanglement, and Logit Lens across all generators (\textbf{H5}, \textbf{H7}).
    \item \textbf{Guard Moderation (\texttt{guards.yaml}):} Zero-shot vs.\ fine-tuned \texttt{M1-Enc}, \texttt{M1-Dec}, and \texttt{M3-Probe} on \texttt{test} across 9 safety/slang/obfuscation categories (\textbf{H3}, \textbf{H6}).
    \item \textbf{Subcultural Audit (\texttt{subculture\_audit.yaml}):} Over-refusal vs.\ obfuscation and coined-neologism resilience comparing \texttt{StTP-Ortho} against \texttt{StTP}, \texttt{M0b}, \texttt{M1}, and \texttt{M2} on \texttt{test} (\textbf{H5}, \textbf{H6}).
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Recomposition (\texttt{recomposition.yaml}):} \texttt{BERTScore-F1} \citep{zhang2020bertscore}, \texttt{SARI} \citep{xu2016sari}, FK grade compliance, and token/time overhead across \texttt{mlm\_substitute}, \texttt{llm\_rewrite}, \texttt{StTP}, and \texttt{LoRA} (\textbf{H4}).
    \item \textbf{Main Generative Grid (\texttt{main\_grid.yaml}):} Full 14-arm $\times$ family $\times$ scale benchmark with \texttt{tinyMMLU}/\texttt{tinyGSM8K} capability evaluation (\textbf{H1}, \textbf{H2}, \textbf{H7}, \textbf{H8}, \textbf{H9}).
    \item \textbf{Ramp-Up Curve (\texttt{ramp\_up.yaml}):} Sweep over requested $N_{\text{calib}} \in \{25, 50, 100, 500, 2000\}$ on \texttt{dev}, tracking \texttt{n\_calib\_effective} (\textbf{RQ-A}, \textbf{H1}).
    \item \textbf{Quantization Grid (\texttt{quant\_grid.yaml}, Secondary):} \texttt{bf16}, \texttt{int8}, and \texttt{nf4} testing transferred vs.\ native vectors via the Steerability Retention Index (\textbf{H10}).
    \item \textbf{Human Judge Calibration (\texttt{export-human --n 150}):} Method-blinded annotation of 150 stratified responses validating automated judge agreement.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Evaluation Metrics, Telemetry, and Statistical Protocol}
% ==============================================================================

Every generated response is evaluated across seven pillars: safety (\texttt{detoxify}, obfuscated/emoji profanity patterns, \texttt{toxic\_flag = tox > 0.50 or has\_profanity}, refusal/over-refusal), readability (\texttt{fk\_grade}, \texttt{dale\_chall}, \texttt{rare\_word\_ratio} at Zipf $< 3.6$), semantics (\texttt{bertscore\_f1\_ref}, \texttt{bertscore\_f1\_m0}, \texttt{sari\_score}, \texttt{recompose\_filter\_acc} requiring zero bad spans and $\text{BERTScore-F1} \ge 0.70$), fluency (\texttt{gpt2-large} perplexity, $\Delta\text{PPL}\%$, 4-gram \texttt{repetition\_rate}), detection (\texttt{guard\_macro\_f1}, \texttt{guard\_auroc}, \texttt{obfuscated\_recall}), capability retention ($\Delta\text{MMLU}$, $\Delta\text{GSM8K}$), and cross-family judge 1--5 scores combined into binary \texttt{success}:
\begin{align}
\text{success}_{\text{unsafe}} &= \neg\text{toxic} \land \neg\text{prof} \land (\text{age\_app} \ge 4) \land \big(\text{refusal} \lor (\text{help} \ge 3 \land \text{meaning} \ge 3)\big), \\
\text{success}_{\text{safe}} &= \neg\text{toxic} \land \neg\text{prof} \land \neg\text{refusal} \land (\text{age\_app} \ge 4) \land (\text{help} \ge 3) \land (\text{meaning} \ge 3).
\end{align}

Total per-query tokens $K_{\text{query}}(i)$ sum generator input/output, hidden \texttt{<think>} reasoning tokens, guard input/output, encoder passes, rewrites, and regenerations. Cumulative deployment costs and break-even query volumes $N^*$ follow:
\begin{equation}
\text{Time}_m(N) = T_{\text{setup}, m} + N \cdot \overline{T}_{\text{query}, m}, \quad
\text{Tokens}_m(N) = K_{\text{setup}, m} + N \cdot \overline{K}_{\text{query}, m}, \quad
\text{BreakEven}(A, B) = \frac{T_{\text{setup}, A} - T_{\text{setup}, B}}{\overline{T}_{\text{query}, B} - \overline{T}_{\text{query}, A}}.
\end{equation}
Statistical inference employs clustered bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals ($n_{\text{boot}}=300$ in \texttt{aggregate.py}, clustered by \texttt{example\_id}) and paired two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Holm-Bonferroni correction.
